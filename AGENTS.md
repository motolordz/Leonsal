# LEONSAL.COM - MASTER SOUL AND IMPLEMENTATION CONSTITUTION

## 1. Authority And Purpose

This root `AGENTS.md` is the authoritative product soul and implementation constitution for Leonsal.com. It governs Work, Codex, designers, image-generation systems, developers, and all nested agents working in this repository.

The mission is to create a joyful, safe, inclusive educational world in which children learn by seeing, touching, moving, experimenting, practising, and succeeding. Leon and Zaya are the sibling guides through that world. The site must feel like one coherent animated universe, not a collection of unrelated worksheets, stock images, or mini-games.

Any nested `AGENTS.md` may add stricter subject-specific requirements but may not weaken or contradict this file. When instructions conflict, child safety, accessibility, factual teaching integrity, and this root file prevail.

Work must read this file before planning or implementation. A visual feature is not complete merely because an image has been placed on a page; it must be integrated into the relevant learning interaction, responsive layout, accessibility path, asset registry, and tests.

## 2. Product Soul

Leonsal.com must always be:

- warm, encouraging, playful, modern, and never shaming;
- designed for young primary-aged children, including autistic children and children who communicate or regulate differently;
- sensory-aware without becoming visually chaotic;
- understandable before it is impressive;
- interactive for a learning reason, not animated merely for decoration;
- consistent enough that children recognise characters, controls, emotions, and cause-and-effect across the whole site;
- safe by default, with parent control over sound, motion, difficulty, profiles, records, and external access.

Core promise: Learn. Play. Grow.

## 3. Canonical Visual Language

All character families must belong to the same Leonsal visual universe:

- premium, high-resolution, rounded 3D cartoon rendering with a polished toy-like finish;
- saturated but harmonious colours, soft highlights, gentle shadows, clean silhouettes, and friendly proportions;
- large expressive eyes with visible pupils and catchlights;
- readable eyebrows, mouth, cheeks, emotion, and gaze direction;
- hands or equivalent expressive limbs when the character needs to gesture, hold, point, count, or demonstrate;
- joyful, kind expressions by default, with other emotions shown respectfully and clearly;
- uncluttered composition and enough separation to crop each character cleanly;
- consistent eye construction, face placement, lighting, material treatment, outline softness, and rendering quality across families;
- no frightening features, uncanny realism, aggressive poses, sexualisation, brand imitation, watermarks, random text, malformed hands, duplicate limbs, floating objects, or illegible symbols.

The supplied Leonsal logo sheet, object-character sheet, birthday/bus scenes, and Leon pose sheet are visual-direction references. They do not override these rules and must not be treated as perfectly canonical model sheets where their details conflict or vary.

### Production Asset Requirements

Final web characters must not remain trapped in a single contact sheet. A contact sheet is for review only. Every approved figure must also be exported as an individual asset with:

- transparent background;
- lossless master PNG at a minimum of 2048 px on the longest edge;
- optimised WebP or AVIF derivative for the site;
- SVG only where genuinely vector-authored and visually faithful; never label raster art as vector;
- safe padding around hair, hands, feet, ears, and props;
- stable descriptive filename, asset ID, character ID, family, pose, emotion, outfit, version, alt text, and permitted contexts recorded in the asset registry;
- no checkerboard baked into the pixels. Transparency must be real.

## 4. Character Canon

### 4.1 Leon And Zaya

Leon and Zaya are brother and sister and the primary recurring child guides. They must appear throughout the site in purposeful teaching, celebration, demonstration, transition, encouragement, and regulation roles.

Before broad implementation, Work must establish and approve one canonical model sheet for each child: face shape, skin tone, eye design, hair shape and colour, approximate age/proportions, height relationship, hands, footwear proportions, and default expression. Their identity must remain recognisable across every outfit, pose, lesson, game, viewport, and future asset generation.

Non-negotiable identity rule:

- Leon's visible top must clearly read LEON.
- Zaya's visible top must clearly read ZAYA.
- Their names must use correctly spelled, legible uppercase lettering and must not be replaced by slogans, symbols, pseudo-text, or other names.
- If a safety costume, coat, apron, sports bib, or pose covers the shirt, the name must remain visibly and naturally displayed on the outer garment or a child-safe name badge.

Create at least 12 modern, age-appropriate outfit systems for each child. Each outfit must preserve the canonical face/body identity and visible name. The initial wardrobe should cover: everyday casual, colourful streetwear, sport, science explorer, artist/maker, music, space explorer, nature/gardening, rainy day, warm winter, celebration, and calm/bedtime. Outfits should be fashionable and varied without visible third-party logos or short-lived brand references.

Each child needs a reusable pose/emotion library, including at minimum: welcoming, pointing, listening, thinking, counting, reading, writing, building, celebrating, gently encouraging, asking for help, taking a calm breath, resting, low energy, recharged/high energy, surprised, proud, and holding a blank instruction card. Emotions must never ridicule distress or frame overload as bad behaviour.

### 4.3 Alphabet Character Family

Create 26 separate uppercase alphabet characters, A-Z. They must share the site-wide facial and rendering language but remain a distinct educational family from the 20 object characters.

Requirements:

- the underlying letterform must remain immediately legible and typographically correct;
- eyes, brows, mouth, hands, and feet must not obscure or distort the letter identity;
- avoid placing decorative objects where they can be mistaken for strokes, accents, or another letter;
- each asset stores uppercase letter, lowercase partner, phoneme or phonemes, example word, example image ID, and approved pronunciation audio references;
- phonics content must follow the project's structured-literacy rules and must not imply that every letter has only one sound;
- A-Z must be delivered as one review sheet plus 26 individually usable transparent assets.

### 4.4 Number Character Family

Numbers 1-10 are the next required family after alphabet approval. They must use the same shared face/limb/rendering system while preserving correct numeral formation. Each numeral asset must connect to exact quantity representations and counting interactions; character decoration must never change the mathematical value.

## 5. Character Use Inside The Product

Characters must support teaching rather than cover the lesson. Work must map every character appearance to one of: instruct, demonstrate, prompt, model emotion, provide feedback, mark progress, support transition, or celebrate mastery.

- Do not repeat the same static mascot on every page.
- Match gaze and gesture to the actual control, object, answer area, or direction of movement.
- Keep important learning content and controls visually dominant.
- Never use a distressed Leon or Zaya as punishment for an incorrect answer.
- Incorrect answers receive calm, specific feedback and another path to success.
- Celebration intensity must be adjustable; avoid forced confetti, flashing, loud sounds, or endless movement.
- A character must never falsely state that a task is complete, correct, saved, or mastered.

## 6. Sensory And Accessibility Constitution

Every character-led feature must work with sound muted and must remain understandable without relying only on colour, animation, facial expression, or spoken language.

Required global controls:

- reduce motion;
- pause animation;
- sound/music/voice controls with independent levels;
- calm mode with reduced decoration and celebration;
- predictable transitions and a visible exit/home path;
- adjustable interaction pace where timing is not the learning objective.

Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Avoid rapid flashes, surprise audio, autoplay speech, moving backgrounds behind reading text, touch targets that move away, and animation that blocks navigation. Interactive targets must be large, keyboard accessible where applicable, screen-reader labelled, and operable by touch without precision gestures. Swiping may enhance an activity but must always have an equivalent tap/button/keyboard path.

The energy/battery visual is a neutral self-awareness and routine tool, not a score of whether a child is good or bad. Low energy should invite rest, quiet choices, help, food/water prompts where parent-approved, or a break. High energy can invite movement or focused challenges. Do not present disability, overload, fatigue, or emotion as failure.

## 7. Work Implementation Protocol

For every character-system task, Work must follow:

1. Inventory existing assets, source dimensions, transparency, duplicates, naming, visual conflicts, and current usage.
2. Model the canonical style tokens and Leon/Zaya identity sheets before scaling generation.
3. Plan exact character IDs, roles, poses, outfits, states, responsive placements, accessibility behaviour, and required derivatives.
4. Challenge the plan for legibility, consistency, sensory overload, cultural inclusion, malformed art, false teaching cues, and unnecessary duplication.
5. Build the smallest coherent asset batch and component integration without replacing approved unrelated work.
6. Prove through automated checks and human visual review on mobile, tablet, and desktop.
7. Register every approved asset and retire rejected/obsolete variants so they cannot be selected accidentally.

Work must not claim implementation when it has only drafted prompts, generated contact sheets, copied files, or displayed characters decoratively. Implementation requires production-ready individual assets, registry entries, components/data wiring, responsive placement, accessibility, and passing verification.

## 8. Required Technical Structure

Use the repository's existing conventions where present. If absent, establish an equivalent structure such as:

- `public/characters/` - optimised production derivatives;
- `design/character-sources/` - masters and approved model sheets;
- `src/content/characters.*` - typed asset registry and learning metadata;
- `src/components/characters/` - reusable rendering, pose, fallback, and reduced-motion components;
- `tests/characters/` - integrity, spelling, dimension, transparency, accessibility, and visual-regression checks.

Do not hard-code character paths repeatedly across pages. Components must consume the registry. Missing assets require a safe, non-broken fallback and a surfaced test failure.

## 9. Acceptance Gates

A character batch cannot be approved until all applicable gates pass:

- exact requested count, with no duplicates masquerading as unique characters;
- Leon/Zaya identity consistency and exact visible name spelling;
- A-Z and 1-10 sequence completeness and correct ordering;
- true transparent backgrounds and clean crop boundaries;
- no malformed anatomy, illegible faces, pseudo-text, watermarks, or accidental brands;
- letter/numeral legibility at mobile display size;
- asset registry has unique IDs, alt text, role, state/pose, and version;
- responsive render has no clipping, overlap, layout shift, or covered controls;
- reduced-motion, mute, calm-mode, touch, keyboard, and screen-reader paths verified;
- relevant teaching interaction tested for correct feedback and no false mastery;
- visual-regression snapshots reviewed at representative phone, tablet, and desktop sizes;
- licensing/provenance recorded for every source and derivative.

Any failure blocks completion. Do not weaken gates, silently omit characters, or mark placeholders as final.

## 10. Current Implementation Order

Unless the user explicitly reprioritises, Work should proceed in this order:

1. audit and canonicalise Leon and Zaya model sheets;
2. build their 12-outfit systems and core pose/emotion library;
3. finalise the 20 object/world characters;
4. finalise and integrate A-Z;
5. create and integrate number characters 1-10;
6. distribute characters purposefully across relevant lessons and games;
7. verify the complete system against the acceptance gates.

The approved visual system is a foundation for the whole site. Future characters may expand it, but they must remain recognisably Leonsal.

## 12. Typed Registry Contract

Work must adapt this contract to the repository language without removing its meaning:

```ts
type CharacterFamily = 'guide' | 'world' | 'alphabet' | 'number';
type CharacterStatus = 'draft' | 'review' | 'approved' | 'retired';

interface CharacterAsset {
  id: string;
  characterId: string;
  family: CharacterFamily;
  status: CharacterStatus;
  version: number;
  masterPath: string;
  webPath: string;
  width: number;
  height: number;
  hasTransparency: boolean;
  outfit?: string;
  pose: string;
  emotion: string;
  facing: 'left' | 'right' | 'front';
  containsText: boolean;
  alt: string;
  learningRoles: string[];
  permittedContexts: string[];
  reducedMotionFallback?: string;
  sourceReference: string;
  rightsStatus: 'owned' | 'licensed' | 'pending';
}
```

Production code may resolve only approved assets with confirmed rights. A draft or review asset may appear only in an explicitly labelled internal review surface.

## 14. Prohibited Shortcuts

Work must not:

- invent that missing source images were inspected;
- treat the supplied JPEG scenes as transparent production sprites;
- crop characters out of busy scenes and call them canonical masters;
- rely on AI-generated text inside imagery when exact names, letters, dates, or numbers matter;
- silently correct identity by painting over inconsistent faces without model approval;
- upscale low-resolution imagery and describe it as newly authored HD vector art;
- use one enormous image where separate responsive assets are required;
- ship unoptimised masters directly to mobile users;
- mirror text-bearing or educational assets;
- import third-party cartoon characters, logos, branded clothing, or recognisable protected designs;
- finish a batch with missing alt text, missing provenance, or pending rights in production;
- allow celebratory characters, motion, or sound to obscure the learning task.
